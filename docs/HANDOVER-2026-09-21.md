## The one design decision worth keeping

**No new server engine was needed.** `search-library` already does hybrid
recall plus a rerank over the caller's own library, which is what "everything I
have about Paris" is. Both new screens call it with `limit: 40`, and it is the
reason the sheet is not a word search: in the fixture, typing *Puglia* in the
Library matches **2** rows; the sheet finds **7**, including the masseria in
Ostuni and the trattoria in Lecce, from **two different circles**.

## What shipped

### The way in (`libSheetCtaHtml`)
Type three characters in the Library and the results carry **Make a sheet for
"X"**. It is patched in by `libUpdateResultsInPlace`, not only by `renderLibrary`
— typing does not re-render the Library, so a CTA rendered only in the view
function would have appeared a beat late and then stuck at the previous word.

### `topic-sheet` — everything you have about it
Grouped by what the things are (`recCategory`, the owner's own word when they
have set one — 0045), each row saying where it is, who gave it to you and in
whose words, with the Maps / Booking / Google buttons. Head counts the items
and the circles they came from. Two actions at the top: **Send this sheet**,
**Save as a list** (a real `collections` row, so it is in the Library
afterwards).

### `sheet-send` — Recommend's picker, not a second one
Circle chips, everyone ticked, untick to drop, and someone with no number or
email is shown but cannot be ticked — the same `qWhoHtml` / `qChosen` the Ask
and Recommend screen uses. It deliberately does **not** call `qWhoRefresh`:
that also applies the ask screen's degree-2 rule, which would have greyed this
list out for anyone whose last question went to degree 2.

The delivery rows afterwards — including the one-tap WhatsApp hand-off — are
now `collectionSendResultHtml`, one function that both senders call. It was
inside the Library's own handler, and a copy of a WhatsApp hand-off is exactly
the kind of thing that drifts.

### `answer-lib` — answering someone out of your library
Offered on the waiting card on Home and on the Inbox row. What matches their
question arrives **ticked**; untick what you would rather not send; each thing
sent becomes its own answer from you, through their own token. Afterwards the
request is marked answered in the same breath the server marks it (0046).

**It is never a dead end.** When the library has nothing, or the search fails,
the screen says so and offers *Answer in your own words* — the `respond.html`
form that has always worked. That link is also at the foot of the ticked
screen.

### "N of M answered" counts PEOPLE
One person answering with five things is one person. Counting rows would have
read **"5 of 3 answered"** the first time anyone used the new sheet. Distinct
by `contactId`, anonymous answers counted individually; the number of things is
still shown beside it.

## The server

- **`receive-response`** takes `items[]` (cap **20**) or the single answer
  `respond.html` has always sent — one shape from the top of the handler down.
  The first item takes the row the token belongs to; the rest are **inserted**
  beside it, each with its own already-spent `response_token`, because that
  column is `unique not null` (**measured**: `0001_initial_schema.sql` — there
  is no unique on `(query_id, member_id)`, and several answers per person
  already exist in production). A failed **first** write still returns 500 and
  the token stays unspent; a later one is logged and the rest are kept — losing
  four good answers because the fifth clashed would be worse than the gap. One
  notification, however many arrived.
- **`send-collection`** takes `member_ids`, the way `send-query` has since
  v0.88.0. No list means the whole circle, which is what every earlier caller
  meant; a list that matches nobody in the circle is an error rather than a
  silent send to everyone.
- **`search-library`** allows `limit` up to 40 and grows the candidate pool with
  it (`p_limit: Math.max(30, limit * 2)`) — asking the reranker for 40 out of a
  pool of 30 is asking for nothing.

## What is NOT done

- **`docs/BETA-SMOKE-TEST.md` has not been walked on this build.** It is the
  25-minute two-account pass and it is the thing to do before showing anyone.
- **The topic sheet has no memory.** Leaving the screen and coming back
  re-runs the search (~1s). Fine, but it is not cached across views.
- **`showView` clears `AppState.searchQuery`**, so Back from a sheet lands on
  an empty Library search box rather than the word you typed.
- **Nothing tells you a sheet is stale.** A list saved from a sheet is a
  snapshot; new things about the same topic do not join it.
- Still open from 19 Sep: failed deliveries and **Resend** are unreachable once
  you leave the post-send screen (`AppState.queryState` is memory-only); the
  "You're in" circles need a server query; secondary screens are only restyled.

# The invite was broken, and had been all along (0051)

Found while dan asked a question about the **wording** of the invite message:
does the Invite button send the same thing as the shareable link? It does not
(two texts have drifted), but both produce the **same** `/?join=<token>` URL —
`get_or_create_circle_link` reuses one token per circle — and therefore the
same landing page. A personal invite is forwardable, and "Disable this link"
revokes the ones already sent.

Pulling that thread found something much worse.

## What was broken

**Every invite to someone already in the circle failed.** Measured in
production, from dan's own test:

| Time, 21 Sep | |
|---|---|
| 15:27:46 | dan types **naama** into **leros** — whatsapp, `linked_user_id` null, because she had no account yet |
| 15:32:45 | she presses send in WhatsApp; the claim is recorded — template, webhook and her end all fine |
| 15:33:14 | `complete-join` creates her **account** |
| — | the membership insert is refused by `members_person_circle_uniq`; the claim is never consumed, `uses` never increments |

She read **"Couldn't finish signing you in."** For ever: tapping the link again
repeats it exactly. dan was told nothing at all — no notification, and her row
still reads "not on Trustnet", so the obvious move is to press Invite again.

**Why.** Both join paths looked for a member carrying the joiner's *user id*. A
row typed before that person had an account can never carry one, so both always
tried to insert, and the database always refused. The only joins that had ever
worked — 11 of them — were people joining a circle they were **not** already in.
The email-typed variant did not fail; it made a **second** member row for the
same human, because an email key and a phone key are different people as far as
the constraint can see.

## The fix

`join_circle_as_user()` is now the one place a person becomes a member of a
circle. It resolves the **person** through `contact_key` — the same normaliser
the unique index is built on — and **links the row that is already there**,
keeping the owner's own name and contact for them. Idempotent; never takes over
a membership pointing at another account (that returns a reason, not a 500
carrying a constraint name — found by the migration's own dry run); and the
owner never becomes a member of their own circle, which the old code allowed.

`join_circle_via_link` now goes through the same door, its contract to the
client unchanged. `complete-join` v27 calls it instead of its own
select-then-insert — downloaded afterwards and diffed equal to git, `verify_jwt`
untouched.

**Deliberately not done: matching by name.** Two people called Dan Shapiro are
two people. Someone typed in by *email* who joins by *WhatsApp* is still a
second row — honest and visible to the owner, rather than a silent wrong merge.

## Evidence

`join-adopts-sim.js` — 20 checks against the real database, the real trigger and
the real index, inside a transaction that is always rolled back, proving the
environment before asserting anything. Its control restores the pre-0051 path
(`join_circle_via_link` exactly as `0025_recover_functions.sql` defines it, plus
complete-join's own select-then-insert) and **fails 10**, reproducing what naama
hit — including the signed-in half.

`joiner-name-sim.js` had been throwing this same duplicate-key error since
before the session, reporting nothing about the function it exists to test: it
took `circles … limit 1` and inserted a membership into an arbitrary circle. It
makes its own circle now.

## Correction to this file's own advice

CLAUDE.md says three identity triggers are dropped on purpose. **Those are on
`canonicals`.** `trg_member_identity` on `members` is armed, and 0051 depends on
it: it is what gives every member row a `person_id`. I read that line as
covering members and was wrong; the database said otherwise within a minute.
