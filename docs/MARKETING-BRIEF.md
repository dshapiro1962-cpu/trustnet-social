# MARKETING BRIEF — 6 October 2026

Written so a conversation about marketing can start cold and not waste its
first hour rediscovering what is already decided. Everything here is either a
measurement from the live database or a decision dan has already made, with the
reason attached. Nothing in it is aspiration.

If you are a session reading this to help with marketing: **read it before
proposing a line of copy.** Half the obvious ideas have already been tried and
rejected, and section 4 says which and why.

---

## 1 · What the product is

> Trustnet is the channel for reaching out to people you trust for reliable
> information — and recommending back. No public reviews, no algorithmic feeds.

That sentence is dan's own, chosen 5 October. It appears in every invitation,
in the page's `og:description`, and on the support page.

The shorter form, on the sign-in screen:

> Recommendations from the people you trust. No password, no code to type —
> you send one message and you're in.

**The mechanics in one paragraph.** You make a *circle* — a named group of
people you trust, like "Puglia trip" or "Doctors". You ask that circle a
question. They answer from their own phone, without needing an account. Their
answers land in your *library*, which is searchable and yours. You recommend
back. Nothing is public: there is no profile, no feed, no rating from
strangers, and a circle is not shown to the people in it.

## 2 · The numbers, as they actually are

Measured on the live database, 6 October 2026. First account 3 July.

| | |
|---|---|
| accounts | **22** |
| …that have ever saved anything | **8** |
| …that have ever asked a question | **9** |
| circles | 38 |
| people listed as members | 83 |
| …who have themselves joined Trustnet | **53** |
| recommendations saved | 211 |
| questions asked | 81 |
| answers received | 67 |
| **answers still outstanding** | **104** |
| busiest week | w/c 7 September, 47 saves |

**Three things a marketer must not be shielded from:**

**Most accounts are dormant.** 8 of 22 have ever saved anything. Growth is not
the current problem; a reason to come back is.

**The answer rate is 39%** — 67 answers against 171 asks. Someone asks their
circle and three in five get silence. That is the product's central weakness
and no campaign fixes it.

**Invitation-to-join conversion is strong**: 53 of 83 listed people joined.
When someone is personally asked by a friend, they turn up. That is the one
growth mechanic that demonstrably works, and it is the only one the product
has.

## 3 · What people actually use it for

Not what it was designed for. The last twelve questions asked, verbatim:

- *Anyone been to Lecce? Looking for somewhere to eat, early October, two adults.*
- *is there a good ferry connection between kos and leros*
- *is there a beach near a taverna in Krithoni Leros*
- *Would love to get recommendation for hotel in London*
- *good pizza in rome*
- *good hotel in leros* · *good bar in leros* · *good sightseeing spot in leros*
- *חבר נוסע לאזור בארי באיטליה יש לך המלצות*
- *חבר נוסע לאיטליה לאזור Apulia מבקש המלצות למלונות ומסעדות*

**It is a travel product in practice.** Israelis planning trips to Greek
islands and southern Italy, asking friends who have just been. The doctor and
the plumber are in the pitch; they are not in the data. Two of the twelve are
in Hebrew, which is the second thing: **the real user base is bilingual**.

Also worth knowing: **201 of 211 saved recommendations have no category at
all.** The use-case categories the product offers are, in practice, unused.

## 4 · Positioning already tried and rejected

Do not re-propose these. Each was written, shown to dan, and refused for a
reason that still holds.

| rejected | why |
|---|---|
| "Dany added you to their leros circle" | third person about the sender, in a message arriving *from* that sender |
| "no app needed" | said while inviting someone to join an app |
| "Trustnet is a private, network-based recommendation and information retrieval application…" | a brochure, not a person. Compress to a sentence and a half |
| anything naming Google | "no mention of Google" |
| leading on where recommendations are *kept* | "the emphasis should be on people whose recommendations we trust as opposed to public review platforms and AI" |
| "ask people you trust — a doctor, a restaurant, a plumber" | "could be misunderstood as if you trust the doctor". The nouns must hang off the *asking* |
| "Beta" on the version line | changed to **MVP**, 23 Sep |
| putting the film in the menu | "the menue is not the place its buried a new user will not go to the menue" |

**One reversal worth understanding.** On 21 September dan deliberately chose the
concrete nouns — *a doctor, a restaurant, a plumber* — so a stranger could
picture using it. On 5 October he replaced them with *reliable information*,
after reading an invitation on his own phone. The lesson is not that either is
right: it is that **the right words differ between a cold audience and a warm
one**, and the invitation goes to someone a friend has already vouched for.
A marketing audience is cold. The nouns may well be right again there.

**His standing objection to half-measures**, said about an engineering
shortcut but it applies to copy: *"that not a good solution we dont do halfway
solutions its bad practice."*

## 6 · What marketing may not say

These are promises made **in writing** on `/privacy` and `/terms`. Copy that
contradicts them is a liability, not a stretch.

- There is **no public profile, no feed, and no rating from strangers**
- A circle is **not shown** to the people in it
- Trustnet **never messages anyone on your behalf** — the invite opens *your*
  WhatsApp and *you* press send
- Recommendations are **personal opinions**, not professional advice, and
  Trustnet does not check them
- It is **free**, and takes no payment of any kind
- It is an **MVP in private testing**; the terms say plainly that it can break
  and that data can be lost

Also disclosed and therefore unavoidable: pasted chat text is sent to OpenAI,
messages go through Meta's WhatsApp API, and the database is in Singapore.

## 7 · Distribution reality

**There is no app store.** It is a web app you add to your home screen. That
removes the usual launch surface entirely.

**There is no public content.** Nothing to index, nothing to share, no profile
to link to. SEO and social virality are not available to this product by
design — the privacy promise and the growth mechanic are the same decision.

**The only working loop is a personal invitation over WhatsApp**, from a person
to someone who already trusts them. 53 of 83 invitations converted. That is the
entire funnel.

**A Meta connector is under review.** Trustnet submitted an MCP connector to
the Muse directory on 27 September. If approved it would be discoverable inside
Muse and in its Settings → Connectors — the first distribution channel that
isn't a friend's WhatsApp. Unknown timeline, no guarantee, do not build a plan
that depends on it.

## 8 · The questions marketing has to answer

1. **Who is this for, now that the data says travel?** Lean into trips, or
   insist on the general case the pitch describes?
2. **Hebrew or English first?** The user base is bilingual, the product is
   English, the film is both.
3. **What brings someone back?** 8 of 22 accounts are active. Retention is a
   product question that copy cannot answer, but the positioning has to be
   honest about what the app is *for* between trips.
4. **What is the ask?** With no store and no public surface, every campaign has
   to end in someone sending a personal invitation. What makes a member do that?
5. **Does "no algorithmic feeds" mean anything to a normal person**, or is it
   insider framing? It has never been tested on anyone outside this project.
