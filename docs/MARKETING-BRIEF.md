# MARKETING BRIEF — 6 October 2026

Written so a conversation about marketing can start cold.

**Read this first, before anything else in the file: Trustnet has no users.**
Twenty-two accounts, 211 saved recommendations and 81 questions exist in the
database, and **every one of them is a test** — dan's own accounts and a few
friends trying things at his request. Nobody has used this product because they
wanted to. There is no retention to measure, no engagement to analyse, no
behaviour to learn from, and no funnel.

An earlier version of this brief presented those rows as findings. They are
not. If any document, dashboard or conversation offers you a number about
Trustnet usage, it is measuring dan testing his own app.

**We are at zero.** That is the single most important fact in this file, and
most of what follows exists because of it.

---

## 1 · What the product is

> Trustnet is the channel for reaching out to people you trust for reliable
> information — and recommending back. No public reviews, no algorithmic feeds.

dan's own sentence, chosen 5 October. It appears in every invitation, in the
page's `og:description`, and on the support page.

The shorter form, on the sign-in screen:

> Recommendations from the people you trust. No password, no code to type —
> you send one message and you're in.

**The mechanics.** You make a *circle* — a named group of people you trust,
like "Puglia trip" or "Doctors". You ask that circle a question. They answer
from their own phone, **without needing an account**. Their answers land in your
*library*, which is searchable and yours. You recommend back.

Nothing is public: no profile, no feed, no rating from strangers, and a circle
is not shown to the people in it.

## 2 · What is actually known, and what is not

**Known, because it is built and working:**

- The product does what it says. Ask, answer, save, search, recommend back.
- An invitation arrives from *your own* WhatsApp, not from Trustnet.
- Someone can answer a question without ever making an account.
- Sign-in is one message — no password, no code.
- It runs on a phone as a home-screen app.

**Not known, because nobody has tried it:**

- whether anyone outside this project wants it
- what they would use it for
- whether people answer their friends' questions, and how fast
- whether anyone returns a second week
- whether "no public reviews, no algorithmic feeds" means anything to a normal
  person, or is insider framing
- whether the invitation converts — a friend asking is plausible, not proven
- which language the first real users want it in
- what it is worth, if anything

**Nothing in this list can be answered by a campaign.** They are answered by
putting it in front of twenty people who owe dan nothing.

## 3 · The hypothesis worth naming

dan's own testing has been almost entirely **travel** — Greek islands and
southern Italy, some of it in Hebrew. That is not evidence of demand; it is
evidence of what *he* reaches for first, and he is the person who knows the
product best.

It is still the strongest hypothesis available, for a reason that holds up
without data: a trip has a **deadline, a specific question, and a small group
of people who have just been there**. That is the shape Trustnet fits. "Where
should I eat in Lecce next Tuesday" has an urgency that "find me a plumber
someday" does not.

The counter-hypothesis is that travel is seasonal and occasional, and a product
used three times a year never becomes a habit.

**Both are guesses.** They are written down so the first real test can be
designed to tell them apart, rather than a campaign being built on whichever
one sounds better.

## 4 · Positioning already tried and rejected

Do not re-propose these. Each was written, shown to dan, and refused.

| rejected | why |
|---|---|
| "Dany added you to their leros circle" | third person about the sender, in a message arriving *from* that sender |
| "no app needed" | said while inviting someone to join an app |
| "Trustnet is a private, network-based recommendation and information retrieval application…" | a brochure, not a person. Compress to a sentence and a half |
| anything naming Google | "no mention of Google" |
| leading on where recommendations are *kept* | "the emphasis should be on people whose recommendations we trust as opposed to public review platforms and AI" |
| "ask people you trust — a doctor, a restaurant, a plumber" | "could be misunderstood as if you trust the doctor". The nouns must hang off the *asking* |
| "Beta" on the version line | changed to **MVP**, 23 Sep |
| putting the film in the menu | "the menue is not the place its buried a new user will not go to the menue" |

**One reversal worth understanding.** On 21 September dan deliberately chose the
concrete nouns — *a doctor, a restaurant, a plumber* — so a stranger could
picture using it. On 5 October he replaced them with *reliable information*,
after reading an invitation on his own phone.

That is not a contradiction. An **invitation** goes to someone a friend has
already vouched for; they need no convincing that the product is worth a look.
A **cold audience** has nothing. The nouns may well be right again for
marketing, and the invitation is the wrong precedent to copy from.

**dan's standing objection**, said about an engineering shortcut but it applies
to copy: *"that not a good solution we dont do halfway solutions its bad
practice."*

**The film opens on the pain, not the product** — ratings from strangers,

end up nowhere searchable. Silent by design, so it works without sound.

It has **never been recorded for sending on WhatsApp**, and the Hebrew wording
still wants dan's hand rather than a translation. **It has also never been
shown to anyone outside this project** — including to check whether it makes
sense to someone who has not had Trustnet explained to them.

**There is no landing page.** `trustnetsocial.com` is the app: a signed-out
visitor gets the sign-in screen with one line of explanation. If marketing needs
somewhere to send a stranger, it does not exist yet.

## 6 · What marketing may not say

Promised **in writing** on `/privacy` and `/terms`. Copy that contradicts them
is a liability, not a stretch.

- No public profile, no feed, no rating from strangers
- A circle is **not shown** to the people in it
- Trustnet **never messages anyone on your behalf** — the invite opens *your*
  WhatsApp and *you* press send
- Recommendations are personal opinions, not professional advice, and Trustnet
  does not check them
- It is **free** and takes no payment of any kind
- It is an **MVP in private testing**; the terms say plainly that it can break
  and that data can be lost

Also disclosed and therefore unavoidable: pasted chat text is sent to OpenAI,
messages travel through Meta's WhatsApp API, and the database is in Singapore.

---

## 7 · Distribution reality

**No app store.** It is a web app added to a home screen. The usual launch
surface does not exist.

**No public content.** Nothing to index, nothing to share, no profile to link
to. SEO and social virality are unavailable *by design* — the privacy promise
and the absence of a growth loop are the same decision.

**The only designed route in is a personal invitation over WhatsApp**, from a
person to someone who already trusts them. Whether that converts is untested.

**A Meta connector is under review.** An MCP connector was submitted to the
Muse directory on 27 September; if approved, Trustnet becomes discoverable
inside Muse. Unknown timeline, no guarantee. **Do not build a plan on it.**

## 8 · The actual first job

Not a campaign. Not a launch. **Twenty real users who owe dan nothing.**

The questions that matter, in order:

1. **Who are the first twenty, specifically?** Named people, not a segment.
   What do they have in common, and what are they about to do that Trustnet
   could help with this month?
2. **What is the one sentence that makes a stranger curious** — not the
   invitation wording, which is for someone already vouched for?
3. **Where do they land?** There is no page for a stranger. Either the film
   becomes it, or one gets built.
4. **What does success look like at this size?** Probably not signups. More
   likely: how many questions get answered, and how fast.
5. **English or Hebrew first?** The product is English, the film is both, and
   the first twenty will decide this for us.

Everything else — positioning tests, channels, content, launch — comes after
there is one real circle of people who are not doing dan a favour.
